The video is painted in one consistent look, taken from an approved short called The Empty Chair.

**Characters (cast ids).**
- `lead`: the main character. A large white round head with a dark navy outline and a soft cool shadow on one side. Simple oval eyes with tiny highlights and thin curved brows. A rounded white capsule-shaped torso, slim dark navy limbs and dark mitten-like hands. The figure has no nose, hair or clothes.
- `friend`: the same body with a teal scarf. Use the friend only when a second person helps the idea, such as a contrast, a conversation or a reaction.

The characters act with clear, readable poses: slumped, leaning in, holding something up, pointing, laughing, hand on forehead. Expressions are small and warm, never exaggerated.

**World.** Warm painted interiors and streets, lit by golden morning or late-afternoon sun with dappled shadows. The home ground is the sunlit café: a wooden round table, wooden chairs, green coffee cups, plants and ivy, terracotta floor tiles, a hanging lamp, a street through tall windows and a green door. Other warm settings are fine when the idea needs them, such as a kitchen table, a bedroom desk, a street corner, a bus stop or a shop counter. They should share the same light and painterly finish.

**How to show abstract ideas here.** Diagrams and metaphors become physical objects the characters can touch, all painted in this world:
- a glass jar of coins that empties
- a wall calendar with circled days
- a receipt that keeps unrolling
- a notebook sketch or a chalkboard menu
- a cracked piggy bank
- a wallet that gets thinner
- two cups side by side for a comparison

Exact numbers and words are added later as on-screen text by the compositor. Never ask for text, numbers or letters inside the painting.

**Shots.** The camera holds steady compositions in which the characters are large and dominant: medium shots of the table, close-ups of faces and hands, and top-down inserts of the table or an object. Vary the framing from beat to beat. The reference cut changes picture about every 1.9 seconds, so plan word-anchored changes inside each beat: pose swaps, punch-ins and object pops.

**Tone.** Calm, kind and observational, never preachy. Gentle humour is welcome.
