CHARACTER ACTING PILOT — THE FIRST PAGE

Create one approximately 8–10-second 9:16 portrait video, 720p or higher, 24 fps or
higher if available. Use the supplied keyframe as the first-frame visual reference.
Premium expressive 2D cartoon animation. Keep the character as the focal point.

CHARACTER LOCK
One minimalist 2D protagonist with a white circular head, two small dark-navy oval
eyes, simple curved eyebrows and a short curved mouth. The white-filled slim torso
has rounded shoulders and a gently flared curved base, with a dark navy outline and
subtle cool side shading. Exactly two navy single-stroke arms and two legs; small
dark hands have readable fingers and a believable grip. No hair, clothing or scarf.
Keep identical proportions, outline weight, face design and white fill throughout.
The silhouette and expressive eyes closely follow the provided reference. Expressions
change facial marks and head pose without redesigning the head. No extra limbs or
motion trails. Limbs move through arcs with stable lengths and planted contacts.

SETTING AND COMPOSITION
A softly illustrated warm evening café/study, natural wood table, muted sage details,
window and greenery. Preserve the supplied image's environment and lighting. Keep it
low contrast behind the face. Medium-close three-quarter view, with head and upper
body dominant. Spend at least 80% of the clip on the character, face and hands. No
scenery-only shot. No opening establishing shot that makes the figure tiny. At most
one brief insert of the hand and pen. Modest camera movement only when motivated by
an action or reaction. No constant digital zoom or idle bobbing.

PROPS AND INITIAL STATE
Exactly one dark teal pen and one open cream notebook. The writing hand holds the pen
just above the right-hand page. The other hand anchors the opposite page. A mug can
remain on the table but is not picked up. No phone. The pen never duplicates, jumps
to the table, changes hands or disconnects from the fingers. All furniture is stable.

TIMED ACTING
[0–2s] Hold the medium-close view. The eyes shift from the page to the pen. The writing
hand hesitates just above the page, eyebrows lift slightly, shoulders tense a little.
Small anticipation, no exaggerated shaking. One natural blink.
[2–4s] A quiet breath releases the shoulders. The head inclines toward the page. The
writing hand lowers the pen tip onto the paper along a small curved path. Other hand
keeps contact with the notebook. The face changes from doubt to concentration.
[4–6s] Two or three short writing strokes driven by wrist and fingers, supported by a
subtle elbow movement. Optional brief insert, under one second. Use small abstract ink
marks, no readable writing. Pen contact and emerging marks coincide precisely.
[6–10s] Return to or hold the face-and-hands view. Eyes scan the marks. A small relieved
smile appears gradually. Shoulders settle, chin lifts slightly, and the pen pauses
above the page still in the same hand. Hold that calm reaction. Do not end on scenery.
If the platform only supports eight seconds, compress the final hold, not the physical
action or anatomy. Complete the action before cutting.

AUDIO
Audio-only narration, no dialogue from the character, no lip sync: "One evening, Ava
stopped waiting. She took a breath… and finally let herself write a bad sentence."
One calm adult male narrator, neutral American accent, warm mid-low register, clear
unforced conversational delivery, soft resonance, light natural breath texture,
measured pace with a short pause after "breath", close dry recording. No announcer
voice, exaggerated drama, reverb or voice changes. Very quiet paper/pen contact under
narration. No intrusive music; prioritize intelligibility. Do not display narration
as text. If voice duration exceeds the clip, omit generated narration and provide
clean visuals for the existing continuous narration track in postproduction.

END STATE
The notebook has a few imperfect marks. Both hands remain physically connected, the
pen is still held, and the character's expression is quietly relieved. Preserve this
state through the final usable frame.

AVOID
Photorealistic humans, 3D humanoid character, extra people, clothing, scarf, hair,
redesigned face, rubber arms, extra fingers, detached grips, pen duplication, changing
furniture, teleporting props, scenery dominance, wild camera moves, excessive nodding,
constant bouncing, speech bubbles, subtitles, captions, labels, logos, watermarks.
